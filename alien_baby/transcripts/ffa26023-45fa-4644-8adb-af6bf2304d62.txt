## The relevant passages — most load-bearing first

### 1. Section 8.6 (line 1030) — the single clearest statement on cross-subject reliability
&gt; "Some of the subjects who volunteered for these extremely strenuous experiments found the discomfort unbearable and resigned after a day or two, but those who survived the initial difficulties ultimately reported changes of perception. In every case the positional instability of the perceptual field disappeared gradually, as it had done in Stratton's experiment, and in all but one case the world was ultimately seen the right way round."

This is the invariant-vs-variable answer in one sentence. Taylor reports (a) ATTRITION — some subjects quit in a day or two from discomfort, never reaching adaptation; (b) among completers, the phenomenon is a near-invariant: positional stability returned in EVERY case, and righting of the world in ALL BUT ONE. So the core recalibration is treated as a robust, reproducible outcome across subjects, with one named exception (the 71-day subject, item 3). Why it bears on our replication count: Taylor's own data show the mechanism firing across every subject who genuinely engaged the task; the variation is in whether a subject stays in the task and in one idiosyncratic exception — not in whether the mechanism is real. That argues the "does it replicate" question is less about N and more about whether each replication genuinely engages the adaptation loop (our winnability precondition).

### 2. Section 9.1 (line 1155) — Taylor's explicit N=1 methodology; he rejects group/ANOVA designs
&gt; "These tests differ in an important way from the usual type of psychological experiment, in which different groups of subjects are exposed to different sets of conditions, and the significance of any differences between them is tested by techniques such as analysis of variance. In each of the proposed experiments there is only one subject, and he is exposed to different treatments not at different times or in a succession of different experiments, but simultaneously or, when that is impossible, in alternating periods, within a single experiment."

All three Chapter 9 experiments are single-subject: Experiment I = Papert (line 1166); Experiments II and III = Taylor himself (lines 1191, 1225–1226). Why it bears: Taylor deliberately does NOT establish the phenomenon by many-subject replication and variance testing. His logic of proof is within-subject dissociation — disrupt one subsystem, spare another, in the SAME subject, and show the two go different ways (item 6). For us, this is the theoretical parent of "one positive seed shown to dissociate cleanly" being more probative, in Taylor's own frame, than "N seeds averaged." It does NOT by itself justify one RL seed (a seed is a random init, not a person), but it does say the tradition treats the crucial evidence as within-subject structure, not between-subject counts.

### 3. Sections 8.11 / 8.5 (lines 1045–1047, 1130) — the extreme outlier: the 71-day subject who never perceived the world righted
&gt; (8.11, line 1045) "One of the Innsbruck subjects who wore right-left reversing spectacles for 71 days, and subsequently inverting spectacles for a shorter period, achieved satisfactory behavioral adjustment in both cases but denied that he ever perceived the world the right way round through his spectacles."
&gt; (line 1046) "Erismann and Kohler were puzzled by the failure of this subject to report the righting of the perceptual field that had been found in all other subjects who survived the initial rigors of the experiment, and they devised a variety of ingenious tests... Not one of them succeeded in evoking the expected response."
&gt; (8.5-region, line 1130) "A subject in Innsbruck wore these spectacles for 71 days but failed to perceive the world as correctly oriented, although it was completely stable."

Note the crucial split: the subject's BEHAVIOR adapted normally ("satisfactory behavioral adjustment," field "completely stable"); only his verbal/perceptual report failed to right. Taylor attributes this NOT to a weaker adaptation mechanism or genetic difference, but to a specific idiosyncratic strategy: the subject anchored his verbal reports to an artificial, invariant frame of reference (edges of the visual field defined relative to his own forehead/nose/temples — parts never in view), so his naming responses stayed conditioned to that irrelevant frame and never got re-conditioned to true object positions (line 1045, 1047). Why it bears: this is the one documented failure-to-adapt in the whole corpus, and Taylor reads it as a CONTEXTUAL-PARAMETER artifact (a stray reference frame), not as evidence that adaptation is unreliable. The lesson for replication: a "non-replicating" case can be a measurement/readout artifact (the subject adapted; the report didn't), so a null needs its readout audited before it counts as failure-to-adapt — directly analogous to our "outcome flag can't tell directed from incidental" rule.

### 4. Section 8.2 (line 1006) — Taylor's general theory of WHY subjects vary (this is the book's index entry "Individual differences, 164-65")
&gt; "Behavioral errors that are of primary importance in one subject may be of negligible significance in another, so that the details of behavioral adaptation are likely to vary from one subject to another. ... when it becomes necessary to test our theory by experimenting on mature subjects it is no longer permissible to neglect individual differences. ... the experimental subject is a highly complex multistable system that has long ago completed the adaptations discussed in the previous chapters, and has gone on from there to further stages of adaptation that tend to vary more and more extensively. ... To take an obvious example, a man who leads a sedentary life will be handicapped in a very different way from one who is constantly active, and the course of adaptation to one and the same transformation may therefore be expected to be different in the two cases."

Taylor's attribution of variance, stated as principle: mature subjects differ because their prior adaptations and their everyday DRIVES/HABITS differ, so DIFFERENT SUBSYSTEMS get motivated to readapt in different people. He is explicit that the same transformation runs a different COURSE (which subsystem, how fast, via what route) in different subjects — while (per item 1) the endpoint stays reliable. Why it bears: variability is expected in the DETAILS (route, rate, which subsystem) and is treated as lawful and explainable from each subject's habits — not as noise to be averaged out over many subjects. This reframes "how many replications" as "have we covered the range of drive/engagement regimes," not "have we hit a p-value."

### 5. Sections 9.11–9.12 (lines 1182, 1187) — different TIMESCALES across subjects, attributed to training not constitution
&gt; (line 1182) adaptation "was more rapid than it had been in the two previous experiments in Innsbruck, and this can doubtless be attributed to the systematic training described above."
&gt; (line 1187) "It is significant that the first experience of perceiving an object in its true position occurred on the eighth day of the experiment, whereas the subjects of the two Innsbruck experiments did not report such experiences until they had been wearing the spectacles continuously for more than two weeks. But in their case the choice of the subsystem that was to be active at any moment was determined not by any specific theoretical considerations... The subjects were, so to speak, always biting off more than they could chew."

Concrete between-subject timescale spread: righting on day 8 (Papert, trained/intermittent) vs. &gt;2 weeks (two Innsbruck subjects, untrained/continuous). Taylor attributes the difference to the TRAINING REGIME and task-decomposition, NOT to subject-intrinsic ability. Why it bears: differences in adaptation SPEED between "subjects" are largely a function of the setup (how the task is staged), which maps onto our winnability/curriculum controls — a slow or failing replication may indicate under-decomposed task design, not a true negative.

### 6. Section 5.15 (line 505) — the within-subject dissociation as the load-bearing proof
&gt; "In situations in which stimuli of the form [G] were active, true perception of the horizontal was restored; while in situations involving (G) but not demanding responses adjusted to the gravitational axis, distorted perception of the horizontal persisted. These divergent results were obtained not in two different subjects but in one and the same subject."

Why it bears: the strongest evidence in Taylor's program is two OPPOSITE adaptation outcomes in ONE subject, decided by which subsystem carried a drive. This is the methodological heart of item 2: the tradition's gold standard is a clean dissociation inside one system, which is far more convincing than a between-subject average — relevant to how we weigh "one seed that dissociates cleanly" vs. "many seeds pooled."

### 7. Chapter 5 (line 466) and 7.4 (line 813) — supporting notes on lawful individual variation
- Line 466: rate of an adaptive acquisition "will naturally vary with the frequency of the opportunities provided"; Watson's infants first held the head steady anywhere from "two to fifteen days" — a &gt;7× spread attributed to opportunity/practice, not innate schedule. (Developmental, not prism, but it is Taylor's template for reading rate-variance as opportunity-driven.)
- Line 813 (7.4): "Some subjects, when asked to observe parallactic displacement during locomotion, report that they can detect no such movement" — some from an "exceptionally steady gait," some needing training to perceive it. A documented case of a perceptual capacity present in some subjects and initially absent in others, explained by behavior/experience, not constitution.

## Behavioral principles surfaced (the reusable theory, flagged per standing rule)
- **N=1, within-subject dissociation over between-subject averaging** (9.1, 5.15): the tradition proves recalibration by disrupting one subsystem and sparing another in the SAME organism, not by group statistics. Our replication design should prize clean within-agent dissociations, not just seed counts.
- **Contextual parameter / stray reference frame as a failure mode** (8.11): a subject can adapt behaviorally yet report no perceptual change because a report is conditioned to an irrelevant invariant frame. A "null" can be a readout artifact — audit the measurement channel before calling a replication a failure.
- **Response-specificity / subsystem independence** (9.5, 9.10–9.12): adaptation in one subsystem does not guarantee another; which subsystem adapts, and how fast, depends on where the DRIVE is. Between-subject variance is largely variance in which subsystems are motivated.
- **Setup-not-subject attribution of variance** (9.11–9.12, 5.2, 466): speed/route differences are attributed to training regime and opportunity, echoing the project's winnability rule — a failing replication first indicts the setup, not the agent.
- **Attrition is real and separate from failure-to-adapt** (8.6): some subjects quit before the mechanism can run; that is not the mechanism failing. Distinguish "didn't engage the task" from "engaged and didn't adapt" — the exact separation the winnability rule demands.
- **Endpoint invariant, route variable** (8.6, 8.2): the core outcome (instability clears; world rights) is a near-invariant across completers; the DETAILS vary lawfully by habit/drive. The phenomenon is robust; its trajectory is idiosyncratic.

## Honest gaps — where the local shelf runs thin
- **No quantitative between-subject distribution.** Taylor never reports N-of-subjects with per-subject adaptation magnitudes, variances, or a table you could compute an effect size or replication-power estimate from. His evidence is qualitative and case-based ("in every case," "all but one," "day 8 vs. &gt;2 weeks"). If you need a numeric answer to "how many independent replications does 'replicated' require," the corpus does not supply it — that is a lit-scout question for the modern prism/dual-adaptation psychophysics literature (Welch; Redding &amp; Wallace; Held), which does report inter-subject variance and often uses small-N-with-repeated-measures designs.
- **The outlier is N=1 and second-hand.** The 71-day non-adapter is the corpus's only failure case, reported via Erismann &amp; Kohler, and Taylor explains it away mechanistically. There is no discussion of base-rate of such failures, so you cannot estimate from Taylor how often a genuine non-adapter appears.
- **Individual genetic/constitutional differences are explicitly bracketed** (8.2: "We have taken no account of individual differences in genetic equipment"). Taylor treats variance as drive/habit/training-driven and sets aside constitutional variance by design — so the corpus is silent on innate between-subject differences in adaptability.
- **No treatment of statistical replication philosophy at all.** Taylor's frame is the opposite of frequentist replication; he argues from risky within-subject predictions ("exposes the theory to the risk of being disproved," 9.1). So the corpus informs HOW to think about a single strong dissociation, but offers nothing on multi-seed replication counts as such.

Bottom line for the replication-count decision: Taylor treats the core recalibration as a robust, reproducible invariant (item 1) whose ROUTE and RATE vary lawfully by drive and training (items 4, 5), proven by within-subject dissociation rather than between-subject N (items 2, 6), with the one documented "non-replication" being a readout artifact of a stray reference frame rather than a true mechanism failure (item 3). The corpus gives strong qualitative support for weighting a clean within-agent dissociation heavily, and strong support for auditing any null's readout — but it gives no number, and cannot answer "how many seeds" quantitatively. That number must come from the lit-scout's sweep of the modern prism-adaptation literature.</result>

### Q2 — What is the accepted replication bar for a claim of this kind in ML/RL? Is single-seed → "add a couple seeds" acceptable?

**VERDICT: SOLVED (there is a clear community standard) — and single→"a couple" is BELOW it.** The deep-RL methodology literature is explicit and unforgiving here: 1–3 seeds is considered unpublishable-grade, and even 5 is known to give inflated false-positive rates.

**EVIDENCE:**
- **How Many Random Seeds? Statistical Power Analysis in Deep RL** — Colas, Sigaud &amp; Oudeyer (2018). https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08295 — Concretely: a bootstrap test "should not be used with less than 20 samples," and N=5 empirically produced ~10% false positives against a nominal 5%; they recommend power analysis and using *more* seeds than the analysis prescribes. This is the sharpest answer: for a two-condition comparison, think ~10–20 independent runs, not 2–3.
- **Deep Reinforcement Learning that Matters** — Henderson et al. (2018, AAAI). https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06560 — Demonstrated that *the same algorithm* trained with different seeds can differ *significantly*, and that cherry-picking few seeds fabricates significance; established multi-seed reporting with variance as the field norm.
- **Deep RL at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice** — Agarwal et al. (2021, NeurIPS). https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264 — The current best-practice standard: report **interval estimates (stratified bootstrap 95% CIs)** and robust aggregates (**interquartile mean**), not point estimates, precisely because few-run studies are statistically fragile. - **AdaStop: adaptive statistical testing for sound comparisons of Deep RL agents** — Mathieu et al. (2023). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.10882 — A sequential-testing method that tells you the *minimum* number of seeds to run to reach a valid decision, so you can stop early if the effect is large — directly usable for "how many fresh chains do we actually need?"

**SO WHAT FOR AB:** Your monitor already flagged single-seed idiosyncrasy as the live threat, and the RL literature backs that hard. The defensible target is **not** "one plus a couple." ### Q4 — Established pitfalls: could "adaptation" instead be ordinary policy relearning or a non-spatial salience/phototropic reflex? Is there standard discriminating methodology?

**VERDICT: SOLVED — the motor-control field has mature, named tools for exactly this confound, and you should adopt them.** Your instinct (aftereffect is the discriminator) is correct and is the field's cornerstone, but there are stronger, standardized variants.

**EVIDENCE:**
- **Aftereffect / no-feedback probe blocks** are the canonical discriminator: after learning, remove feedback and instruct "reach straight to the target" — residual mis-reach = *implicit recalibration*, and its decay is the signature that vision was re-mapped rather than a strategy switched on. (Morehead et al. 2017, above.) For AB the analogue is the lens-off probe — which your REPORT_prism_plain.md already identifies as the whole game.
- **Error-clamp / clamped-feedback method** — Morehead et al. (2017), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5505262/ — Feedback is *decoupled* from the agent's actual movement (fixed offset regardless of what it does), removing task-success as a driver, so any residual adaptation *must* be implicit recalibration, not reward-chasing or re-aiming. This is the strongest way to prove the change is spatial recalibration and not "found a policy that scores." An AB analogue would be a ghost whose displacement does not depend on AB's own aim.
- **Explicit-vs-implicit decomposition** — Taylor, Krakauer &amp; Ivry (2014, *J. Neuroscience*). https://www.jneurosci.org/content/34/8/3023 — Introduced report-based separation of volitional re-aiming (analogue: policy relearning / strategy) from implicit adaptation. - **Savings can be a false friend** — Codol, Holland &amp; Galea (2018) "Rethinking motor learning and savings…" https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3134523/ and "Interference underlies attenuation upon relearning" (eNeuro 2025), https://www.eneuro.org/content/12/6/ENEURO.0132-25.2025 — Faster relearning ("savings") is *not* proof of internal-model recalibration; it can be model-free reuse of a rewarded action. Warning: don't treat quick re-adaptation as evidence of genuine visual recalibration.

**SO WHAT FOR AB:** You already have the two best cheap discriminators wired in — the **vision-ablation control** (blinding abolishes the effect → not touch/collision homing) and the **sign argument** (ghost-fraction goes *down*, opposite to what better acuity or phototropism would give). ## What a "chain" and a "seed" actually are

Every trained creature in this project starts from a random number — the **seed** — that sets its initial random weights and the random order it encounters situations. Same setup, different seed = a genuinely different individual that learned the same task its own way. In machine learning a seed is the closest thing we have to "another subject" in a human experiment.

A **chain** here means the full pipeline that produces one adapted creature: train the locomotion body → train the grounded eye on top (Stage 1) → adapt that eye under the prism (Stage 2). One chain = one creature that went through the whole prism experience. Right now we have exactly one such chain (the s0 result). "How many fresh chains" = how many independent creatures we grow through that same pipeline to check the result wasn't a fluke of that one individual.

## Why one isn't enough — the specific thing extra chains rule out

Our single creature showed the effect. The worry is that the effect is a quirk of *that creature's particular wiring*, not a property of the mechanism. A second creature that shows the same thing rules out "we got lucky with one random draw." Eight creatures that mostly agree let you say "this is what the mechanism reliably does, and here's the spread." The field's whole objection to few-seed results is that with one or two draws you literally cannot tell a real effect from a lucky one — the published demonstrations (Henderson 2018; Colas 2018) show the *same* algorithm on different seeds landing in visibly different places, so a claim resting on 1–3 seeds is treated as unproven by construction.

## The part that "fresh" hides: independence has levels

This is the crux of "what are we talking about," because not all extra chains are equally convincing. There's a ladder, cheap-and-weak to expensive-and-strong:

- **Level 1 — same eye, new adaptation seed.** Take the *existing* trained eye, adapt it under the prism again with a new random seed. Tests: "given this specific eye, does the adaptation reliably show up?" Weakness: all these creatures share one eye, so they're correlated — a quirk of that eye repeats in every one. ~3 hours each. - **Level 3 — fresh everything.** Retrain the locomotion body from scratch too, new seed all the way down. Tests the phenomenon across genuinely independent creatures — the real thing the field's 8–20 assumes. ~15+ hours each, because retraining the crawler base is itself a long run.

When the literature says "8–20 seeds," it means 8–20 *independent* samples — Level 2 at minimum, Level 3 to be airtight. Level 1 seeds don't count toward that number; a reviewer pools them as one correlated cluster.

## First, the direct answer: no, proprio isn't frozen — and there's no "proprio weight block" to freeze

In the current architecture (`StereoCrawlerCNN`), proprioception is **passed in raw**. Look at what the encoder actually returns:

```
forward(obs):  return concat[ proprio_raw , pixel_latent ]
```

Proprio (body position, joint angles/velocities, vestibular) is sliced straight out of the observation and handed to the policy untouched. There's no proprio sub-network with its own weights sitting between the sensors and the policy. The only place proprio gets "processed" is inside the shared policy network (the actor/critic MLP) that reads `[proprio | vision]` and outputs an action. So "freeze the proprio weights" doesn't quite type-check here — there aren't distinct proprio weights; there's one policy that reads both streams.

## Where the "freeze proprio" idea comes from — and why it's a scar, not the current design

Your mental model ("build proprio, then add vision, freeze the base") describes the **old** approach (the v2/v3 line). And it's exactly where we got burned. The finding on record, 2026-05-07: *"Vision training is not failing to help — it is actively destroying a working 60% blind policy. A 12× regression, consistent across 5 runs."* Layering vision onto a proprio base — whether you froze the base or not — kept wrecking the crawl. Freezing hurt; not freezing let vision's bad gradients drag proprio down. That whole era is the reason the question feels dangerous, and it should.

## How the current rebuild protects the base instead: gradient walls, not freezing

The prism rebuild solves "don't let the two subsystems corrupt each other" a different way — it walls off the *gradients* between them, in both directions:

- **Reward can't touch the eye.** During the reward update (`MismatchPPO.train`), the vision encoder is frozen — PPO trains only the policy/value heads. So reward can never reshape the eye into a non-visual shortcut.
- **The eye's grounding signal can't touch the motor policy.** The eye is trained by a *separate* optimizer that edits *only* the encoder's weights (the CNN + projection + bearing head). The "see-vs-touch mismatch" gradient never reaches the policy MLP that drives the crawl.

So each subsystem is frozen exactly while the other one is learning, and they take turns: between rollouts the eye is tuned by the mismatch signal; during the reward step the eye is frozen and the motor policy is tuned by reward.

## So how is "AB needs its proprio base" honored?

Two ways, and neither is freezing:

1. **The crawl/motor policy keeps being rewarded through every stage** — grounding the eye (Stage 1), adapting under the lens (Stage 2). It's *preserved by continuing to be reinforced*, so it doesn't rot. It's plastic, not frozen — but it's plastic to the *right* signal (reward for good crawling), never to the eye's grounding signal.
2. **The wall keeps the eye honest**, so that when the policy does start leaning on vision, it's leaning on a *true* bearing (the eye was trained to report where the ball actually is, confirmed by contact), not on noise that would drag the crawl the way old vision did.

## The honest residual risk

The wall protects the eye's *honesty*, but it can't *force* the policy to use the eye. The policy is still free to reweight how much it leans on proprio vs. vision under reward. That's the exact door the earlier decoy work fell through — the "Story B" failure where, under the shift, AB **dropped vision and ran a rote motor habit** instead of re-aiming by sight. The grounded-eye design is the bet that a genuinely honest bearing signal gives the policy a *reason* to keep using vision rather than abandon it. That's what the whole prism test is ultimately checking.

One footnote for completeness: there *is* a `--freeze-encoder` flag, but it's a diagnostic — you freeze the eye to localize whether an adaptation lives in the eye or in the policy heads. It's not part of the normal training path; the default Stage-2 keeps the eye plastic to the mismatch signal, which is what lets it recalibrate under the lens.

&gt; "Now all the elements of Q have been conditioned to the same reaching response... if we **add the angle of the head** implied by the proprioceptive function, [η], **to the y coordinate of the retinal image** implied by the retinal function, [ρ], we get the same answer... from every element of [the equivalence class]. ### 2. The invariant is defined relative to the BODY, and varies relative to head and eyes. §9.20, line 1206
&gt; "the invariant response that is conditioned to all the elements of an equivalence class of Q is **a movement directed to a point that has a specific position relative to my body but constantly varies relative to my head and eyes.**"

The equivalence class collapses all eye/head positions onto one body-referenced action. The body frame is the anchor; retinal/gaze coordinates are the varying inputs. This is the plain-English version of the §8.12 operator.

### 3. Response-specificity: only the acted-on ground recalibrated; the un-acted-on displacement did NOT. §9.20–9.21, lines 1205–1210, and the result at §9.18/9.21, lines 1200–1201
The wedge-prism produced both a **slope distortion** and a **lateral displacement**. The component paired with a corrective **action** recalibrated (in the body frame); the pure retinal **displacement** with no action attached did **not**. ### 4. Near (acted-on) objects recalibrated; distant (only-looked-at) objects stayed distorted. §9.18/9.23, lines 1203, 1217–1218
&gt; "complete... perceptual stability with respect to an object **within reach of the hands** together with **undiminished instability with respect to objects beyond that range**... impossible if one tries... to account for the perceptual changes... by an appeal to some automatic process of physiological adaptation in the visual system."

&gt; "the correction of the errors induced by the prism must have the effect that the perceived positions of points will be **stabilized. ## Behavioral principles surfaced (reusable theory)
- **Response-specificity / effector-indexed adaptation** — recalibration attaches to the specific corrective action, in that action's frame; components with no corrective action (lateral shift, distant look-only objects) do not recalibrate. (§9.20–9.23, §5.15)
- **Body/gravity frame as the anchor; retinal image as the disturbance** — vision is pulled into the behavior-derived, gravity-anchored frame, not the reverse. (§8.10, §8.9)
- **Posture-dependent transform (gain-field, avant la lettre)** — the stored invariant = retinal coordinate + eye/head-posture; corrections are keyed to eye/head position and do not generalize across posture. (§8.12, §9.29, Papert appendix)
- **Interpenetration / equivalence classes** — many (retinal, posture) inputs collapse to one body-referenced response, producing perceptual constancy; putting on/removing the prism becomes just another member of the class. (§9.11, §9.20)
- **Two-level correction** — fixation/gaze adaptation buys *stability/shape*; only bodily-response adaptation buys *veridical position/scale*. (§9.28, §9.30)
- **Head-centered anchoring = the non-adapter's failure mode** — a frame decoupled from action resists adaptation. (§8.11)

### Q2(a) — The gain-field / basis-function account of retinal × eye/head-position → body/spatiotopic

**VERDICT: SOLVED (as the standard neural-computational theory; it is the textbook answer).**

**EVIDENCE:**
- **"Spatial Transformations in the Parietal Cortex Using Basis Functions"** — Pouget &amp; Sejnowski (1997, J Cognitive Neuroscience). The canonical claim: neurons that multiply a retinal Gaussian by a sigmoid of eye position form a *basis set* from which any coordinate frame (head-, body-centered) can be read out by a linear downstream layer. This is the mechanism your proposal calls the "gain-field bridge." (Review context: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/integrative-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnint.2015.00072/full)
- **"Gain Modulation in the Central Nervous System: Where Behavior, Neurophysiology and Computation Meet"** — Salinas &amp; Abbott (2001, The Neuroscientist). https://papers.cnl.salk.edu/PDFs/Gain%20Modulation%20in%20the%20Central%20Nervous%20System_%20Where%20Behavior,%20Neurophysiology%20and%20Computation%20Meet%202001-3566.pdf — multiplicative gain by posture is *the* general operation the CNS uses to change reference frames; a retinal code with an eye/head-position gain is sufficient to read out a head/body-centered target.
- Andersen/Zipser lineage (eye-position gain fields in area 7a/LIP) is the empirical root these models formalize; the Frontiers review above traces it and notes that going all the way to a **body/allocentric** frame needs *additional* transforms with **neck proprioceptors** (head-on-body) and **vestibular** input — exactly your head-pose composition problem.

**SO WHAT FOR AB:** The brain does *not* pick "retinal OR body." It keeps a retinal code and carries eye/head posture as a **multiplicative side-input**, and a downstream linear read-out produces whichever frame the effector needs. That is the literature's direct answer to your tension: the correct object is neither a retinal target nor a body target but a **posture-conditioned map from one to the other**. Your MuJoCo analogue is: feed the policy/decoder the retinal bearing *and* the head/eye pose (the gain variables), so a body-frame bearing is a learnable linear-ish function of the two. Your own note (proposal §36) that the torso axis is ~90° off the gaze axis is *precisely* the neck-proprioception term the gain-field account says you must supply.

### Q2(b) — Does prism adaptation recalibrate a VISUAL map, a PROPRIOCEPTIVE map, or the visuomotor transform — and where does the modern literature put the shift?

**VERDICT: ACTIVELY DEBATED, but the modern center of gravity says the durable recalibration lives substantially in the PROPRIOCEPTIVE (felt-limb/body) map — vision shifts too, but less and more variably; the two components are partly independent.**

**EVIDENCE (oldest → newest, because the field's answer moved):**
- **Harris (1963), Psychological Review** — the founding "proprioceptive change" account: the aftereffect is largely a change in the *felt position of the hand/limb relative to the body*, not a change in vision. Later work qualified "entirely," but the felt-limb-shift component is real and durable. (Summarized in the neuro-computational review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6018603/)
- **Redding &amp; Wallace — additive-components framework** (e.g. "Components of prism adaptation in terminal and concurrent exposure," Perception &amp; Psychophysics). Total shift ≈ **visual shift + proprioceptive shift** (algebraically additive when uncontaminated by strategy); the split depends on exposure — terminal feedback loads the *visual* component, concurrent feedback and head movement load the *proprioceptive* one. https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03207476
- **"shifts in vision and proprioception are independent"** — Hatada, Rossetti, Miall (2006, Exp Brain Res). The two aftereffect components dissociate, arguing the shift is not a single unified remapping. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00221-006-0381-2
- **Petitet, O'Reilly &amp; O'Shea (2018), Neuropsychologia — "Towards a neuro-computational account of prism adaptation."** Frames adaptation as *spatial realignment of the visual-motor and proprioceptive-motor reference frames* into agreement, with a fast strategic process and a slow "true realignment" process; a Bayesian/state-space model reproduces visual-vs-proprioceptive split, spontaneous recovery, aftereffects. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6018603/
- **Tsay, Kim, Haith &amp; Ivry (2022), eLife — PReMo, "implicit sensorimotor adaptation as a process of proprioceptive re-alignment."** The strongest modern claim: implicit adaptation *minimizes a proprioceptive error* (distance between felt hand and the goal), the **proprioceptive shift saturates at a common ~5°**, and this explains phenomena previously credited to visual-error learning. The shift, on this view, lives in the *sensed-body* map. https://elifesciences.org/articles/76639
  - Corroborating/qualifying 2024: individual proprioceptive acuity predicts adaptation extent (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33656948/), while chronic proprioceptive-loss patients still adapt somewhat (https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/jn.00096.2024) — so proprioception is central but not the *sole* substrate. Honest: this is a live debate, not a closed case.

**SO WHAT FOR AB:** The biology's answer to "where should the recalibration land" is *not* "in the retinal/visual map."
