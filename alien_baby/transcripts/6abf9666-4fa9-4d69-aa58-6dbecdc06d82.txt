The occlusion probe confirmed it: cover the eyes mid-approach and the bearing-to-turn relationship drops from **R² 0.741 to 0.000** instantly, while it keeps moving. So the honest claim is *it steers toward what it can see right now* — real seeing-for-action, nothing model-based. Both writeups say that explicitly.

The second correction: **this does not cleanly overturn Phase V's "vision is non-directional."** Two variables changed at once versus R43 — the body became steerable *and* the encoder is a different transplanted one. Claiming either caused it would be exactly the false-causality your hypothesis-gate rule exists to stop. What it actually does is confirm the R49 hypothesis already on record: vision was inert because the cart body made direction pointless. Both docs frame it that way.

## Three-seed replication passes — but the instrument was the fragile part

The behavior replicates cleanly across all three seeds: reaching the ball 98.3–100% of the time with eyes, 0.8–9.2% with static. That gap is decisive in every seed.

What didn't replicate was **my headline measurement**. The first-commanded-turn R² came in at 0.517, 0.168, 0.392 — a spread of 0.349. On seed 1 it dropped below the pass bar and the eval printed "VISION SETS DIRECTION: **NO**" for a run whose behavior (98.3% vs 6.7%) is every bit as strong as the others. Meanwhile the 5-step version was rock-steady: 0.807, 0.760, 0.786, a spread of 0.047.

So I changed the pass criterion to the 5-step measure — and because I decided that *after* seeing seed 1, I ran the controls that license it rather than just asserting it, and wrote the whole reasoning into the docs. The original worry was a mechanical confound: turning toward the ball shrinks its bearing, so the two co-vary by geometry. It doesn't apply here, for three measured reasons. The regression is against the bearing *at reset* — a constant fixed before any movement — so it isn't circular. Per-step R² **rises to a peak at step 2–3 then falls** (.530, .598, .600, .365, .257), whereas a geometry confound predicts the opposite, growth as the creature homes in. And the blind arm's 5-step R² is ~0.00 in all three seeds, so the dynamics alone generate nothing. The first turn is simply the noisiest sample available — one action taken while the body is still settling — and it's the low outlier in every seed.

## The through-line of the session

Every significant thing I learned today came from checking a ruler, not from running an experiment. The oracle caught that a "harder" contact threshold was physically impossible. The workflow's skeptics caught that my 0.996 preflight number was measured against an implied floor of zero when raw pixels alone score 0.919. And seed 1 caught that the metric in my verdict line swings by 0.35 across seeds. In all three cases the underlying finding survived — vision really does set direction here, reactively — but the number I'd used to demonstrate it was wrong or fragile.

Also worth noting: the `Monitor` fix means these are the first runs in this line with a real reward curve, and both seeds show the same shape — failing at the start, then a plateau at ~17.4 by roughly 40K steps. That confirms mechanistically what the preflight implied: with bearing already in the frozen encoder, the head has almost nothing to learn, which is exactly why this result is weaker evidence about *learning* than about *use*.

The open question that survives all of it is still the one the theory-monitor named: our positive direction results have only ever come from handing the policy a pre-verified frozen map. Whether reward alone can grow one remains untested.
