# Overnight — the night AB first reached

*A story-register retelling of the morning summary in OVERNIGHT_LOG.md.
The engineering record there is the source of truth. This is what it looks
like when you tell the same night with AB at the center.*

---

For weeks, AB has been stuck in the same trick. We had placed it on a small
cart that slides north and south, with two small balls a hand's-width to
either side. AB learned one thing and refused to leave it: hold itself stiff,
let the cart carry its body past the balls, and hope a shoulder or a hip
brushed one in passing. We came to call this the *one move then freeze*
failure. Seven hundred and fifty thousand lives of rehearsal across earlier
phases — Phase D, E, E2, F, F-2k — and AB had never learned to extend an
arm. It had only ever learned to be a stiff object that the world occasionally
bumped into.

Tonight we tried one thing, and it broke the trick open: **we turned AB's
restlessness up high.** Where the earlier rehearsals had paid AB a small
bonus for fidgeting, tonight's paid a much larger one. And we eased AB into
the difficulty instead of dropping it in — beginning each life with the balls
within easy body-sweep range, then quietly moving them outward over the first
five thousand heartbeats until they sat at the offset that had blocked AB
for months.

Something happened. After about fifteen thousand heartbeats — three minutes
of our time, an unknowable stretch of AB's — AB stopped freezing. Its right
arm extended laterally out from its body, toward the red ball, and touched
it. Its body shifted south, and the blue ball came into range of its feet.
Twenty out of twenty measured lives reached both balls cleanly. **The first
real reach in the project.**

We re-rolled the dice that determine how AB's first life unfolds — three
times, three different starting points. AB found its way to the same answer
each time. The peaks varied a little, but the shape of the behavior was
the same. This is not a fluke we can dismiss. It is what reliably happens
when AB's restlessness is set high enough to break the freeze, in a world
that does not place the prize beyond its reach.

We added two small refinements that helped. A bonus paid for joint motion
itself — a structural pressure against stillness — pushed AB's reach
slightly farther. And turning the restlessness back down once AB had found
the better strategy let it consolidate without sliding back into the
lottery of accidental grazes. AB is brilliant for a moment, then forgets;
the trick is to capture the moment while it lasts.

---

Then the harder question. Could AB *see?*

We had built a camera into AB carefully. A small visual cortex behind its
eyes — the part of its mind that might learn to recognize the world. In half
of tonight's rehearsals, AB had eyes. In the matched other half, AB was
blind. If vision was load-bearing — if AB *needed* to see the ball to reach
it — the sighted AB should have outperformed the blind one.

It did not. Across six different worlds we built tonight — balls at one
offset, balls at a harder one, balls placed randomly each life so they could
not be memorized from body-sense alone, balls that vanished if AB took too
long — sighted AB and blind AB performed identically, or blind AB
performed slightly better. When we covered the camera mid-life on a sighted
AB, its behavior did not change. The single-step shift in what its muscles
did when we zeroed out the pixels was 0.009 on a scale of [-1, 1]. Less than
the noise.

The strongest test of the night: we placed the balls in random positions
inside a small box each life, so that body-sense alone could not predict
where to reach. **Sighted AB got 34 out of 40. Blind AB got 36 out of 40.**
Covering the camera on the sighted AB during measurement *improved* its
score by one life.

What this means is plain enough: AB's eyes were open the whole time, but
its mind never learned to look through them. The visual cortex we had built
behind those eyes was driven, by the rehearsal process itself, into noise —
into outputs the rest of AB's mind learned to ignore. The reason is
structural. The body's arm-sweep at high restlessness covers the small
task-space so completely that vision was never on the path between AB and
its reward. The optimizer had no reason to teach the visual cortex
anything, so it taught it nothing.

---

We also learned where AB's body ends. The reaching strategy AB found
generalizes outward — at measurement time, with no further rehearsal, AB
reaches successfully out to about two-thirds of the way past where it was
trained. Beyond that, it falters. At the geometric edge — a third of a meter
from the cart's center — neither sighted nor blind AB can succeed. The arms
simply do not extend that far. That is not a learning problem. That is a
body problem.

We tried to push the reach further by handing the older, wiser AB a harder
world to continue rehearsing in. It did not work, in two distinct ways.
First, the tools we use to save AB's mind dropped its memory of past lives
when we handed it the new body, so it relearned from nothing and forgot
the reach. We fixed that — preserved the memory — and tried again. This
time AB's old memories and its new experiences contradicted each other.
The old memories said "this action wins." The new experiences said "the
same action does not win here." AB, with no way to reconcile them, could
commit to neither. Its stochastic rehearsals stayed high-reward; its
deterministic measure collapsed. If AB could have spoken, it would have
said: *you are telling me to do the thing I already learned, except now
when I do it nothing happens, and I do not know which of you to believe.*

---

So tonight's night has three findings, in order of weight.

**AB can reach.** The trick that has blocked this project since Phase D is
breakable. The lever is restlessness, not reward shaping and not task
design. Three independent starts confirm it.

**AB does not see.** Across every world we built to make seeing necessary,
the visual cortex was driven to noise and the rest of AB's mind learned to
ignore it. The body's reach covers the task without needing vision. To make
AB's eyes matter, we will need to change the world so that the reward
itself depends on where AB has been looking.

**The body has limits.** AB's reach generalizes from where it rehearsed,
but only so far. To push it further, we will need a different body, a
different training algorithm, or a way to ease AB into harder worlds
without its old memories fighting its new ones.

The night ends with AB on its cart, reaching for the red ball with its
right arm, with its eyes open and its mind blind, in a body that knows
three quarters of the world it was placed in.
