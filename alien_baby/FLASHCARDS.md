## Reinforcement learning — algorithm side

Q: What is SAC?
A: Soft Actor-Critic. The RL algorithm used here. Modern off-policy method for continuous-action problems (joint torques). "Soft" because it rewards keeping randomness in the policy, which discourages premature lock-in to one strategy.

---

Q: What is the "actor" in SAC?
A: The neural network that chooses actions. Input is the observation (proprio + pixels); output is a distribution over actions. In this project a small MLP: obs → 256 → 256 → action.

---

Q: What is the "critic" in SAC?
A: A second network that estimates "how good was that action in that state?" — a scalar value. SAC uses two critics and takes the minimum to prevent over-trusting its own value estimates. The actor learns to pick actions the critic rates highly.

---

Q: Why does SAC use two critics?
A: Taking the minimum of two value estimates prevents the actor from over-trusting an over-optimistic critic (a known instability in Q-learning). It's a standard trick called Clipped Double Q-learning.

---

Q: What is a "policy"?
A: The rule that maps observations to actions — the creature's moment-to-moment behavior strategy. In code the policy is the actor network. "Running the policy" = executing that mapping step by step.

---

Q: What is an episode?
A: One trial: reset the environment, let the agent act for up to N timesteps or until termination (e.g., touching the target or falling). One full reset-to-termination run.

---

Q: What is a timestep / step?
A: One full decision cycle: agent sees obs → picks action → env advances physics → returns new obs, reward, done. In this env one step = 5 internal physics substeps of 0.01 s = 50 ms of simulated time.

---

Q: How is "step" different from "sim-timestep"?
A: A sim-timestep is 0.01 s of physics. A step bundles 5 of those = 50 ms. The agent decides every 50 ms; physics is computed every 10 ms.

---

Q: How does "step" differ from "episode"?
A: An episode is a full reset-to-termination run (up to 400 or 2000 steps depending on env). A step is one decision cycle inside an episode.

---

Q: What is a "rollout"?
A: A sequence of timesteps, usually a full episode or batch of episodes. "Roll out the policy" = run it in the env and record what happens.

---

Q: What is a replay buffer?
A: A rolling cache of the last ~50,000 transitions (state, action, reward, next_state) the agent has experienced. SAC learns off-policy: it samples random old transitions to update on, not only the latest episode. This is why the agent keeps improving long after behavior has changed.

---

Q: What is on-policy vs off-policy learning?
A: On-policy = learn only from data generated by the current policy (e.g., PPO). Off-policy = learn from any past data, including old policies' rollouts (e.g., SAC). Off-policy is more sample-efficient but can be less stable.

---

Q: What is a SAC update batch / gradient step?
A: Once per env timestep, SAC pulls 256 random transitions from the replay buffer and does one gradient update on actor and critic. "Batch" here is the 256 samples, not a batch of episodes.

---

Q: What is a checkpoint?
A: A saved copy of the trained model (weights + optimizer state). Can be loaded later to resume training or evaluate. In this project they live under `alien_baby/results/`.

---

Q: What is deterministic vs stochastic evaluation?
A: During training the actor samples actions from a distribution (stochastic) to explore. During eval we use `deterministic=True`, which takes the distribution's mean — stable, repeatable behavior.

---

Q: What is the entropy coefficient (ent_coef / α / "temperature")?
A: SAC's knob for how much randomness it encourages in the actor. Higher = more exploration; lower = more committed actions. SAC can tune it automatically. Logged as `ent_coef`.

---

Q: What is entropy annealing?
A: Starting training with a high entropy coefficient (lots of exploration) and gradually lowering it as training progresses (commit to learned behavior). In Phase XII: 0.5 → 0.2 over steps 15K–30K.

---

Q: What is the Polyak update / target network?
A: A stability trick. The critic has a "shadow" target whose weights slowly drift toward the main critic's (τ=0.005 per update). The main critic trains against the slowly-updating target, preventing runaway feedback loops.

---

Q: What is "warm-start" in training?
A: Initializing a new run from a previous checkpoint's weights rather than from scratch. In this project R40's warm-start is noted as broken because `SAC.load` strips the MICOA training step.

---

Q: What is a curriculum?
A: Gradually increasing task difficulty during training. In Phase XII: ball offset ramps from 0 up to 0.15 m over steps 2000–15000, then locks.

---

Q: What is curriculum lock?
A: The point in training where the curriculum stops ramping and the task is at full difficulty. In Phase XII that's around step 15K. Often where collapse happens because the policy was relying on easier early targets.

## Neural-network plumbing

Q: What is an MLP?
A: Multi-Layer Perceptron. A plain feed-forward neural network: stack of linear layers with non-linearities between them. No attention, no recurrence — just matrix multiplies and ReLUs.

---

Q: What is a Linear layer?
A: A matrix multiply plus a bias vector: `y = W·x + b`. The "weights" W are what training updates.

---

Q: What is ReLU?
A: `max(0, x)` applied element-wise. The most common non-linearity. Without a non-linearity, stacking linear layers would collapse to a single linear map.

---

Q: What is a hidden activation?
A: The intermediate vector produced by a layer. `hidden1` = the output of the first Linear+ReLU. It's the network's internal "picture" of the state at that depth.

---

Q: What is a feature extractor?
A: The part of the network that turns raw observations into a feature vector for the policy to consume. For pure proprio it's just identity (flatten). For vision it's typically a CNN.

---

Q: What is latent space?
A: An abstract vector space the network uses internally to represent state, often lower-dimensional than the raw input. Points in latent space have no direct sensory meaning; they're whatever the encoder learned is useful. MICOA's shared latent Z is 64-dim and represents "world state both modalities are seeing."

---

Q: Why is latent space called "latent"?
A: "Latent" = hidden, not directly observed. The space exists only inside the network; you can't measure it externally. Compare to "observation space" (the raw inputs) and "action space" (the outputs).

---

Q: What does "freezing weights" mean?
A: Setting `requires_grad = False` so the optimizer won't update them. Used to mathematically preserve already-learned behavior while letting other parts train.

---

Q: What is a gradient hook / gradient mask?
A: A PyTorch feature that intercepts gradients flowing back through a tensor. Used here to zero out gradients on proprio columns of the first-layer weight — so even though the tensor is marked trainable, the proprio slice effectively isn't.

---

Q: What is backpropagation?
A: The training loop: run network forward → compute loss → compute gradient of loss w.r.t. each weight → nudge each weight to reduce loss. Repeat.

---

Q: What is the learning rate?
A: The size of each weight-update nudge during backprop. This project uses 1e-4 (0.0001) for v2–v5.

---

Q: What is a loss function?
A: A single scalar measuring how badly the model is doing on a batch. The optimizer minimizes it. SAC's actor loss has task and entropy parts; v5 adds consistency; MICOA adds KL agreement and/or predictive terms.

---

Q: What is MSE (mean squared error)?
A: Average of squared differences between prediction and target. The simplest loss. Used in the v5 consistency loss as `MSE(h_full, h_blind)`.

---

Q: What does "MSE-style" loss penalize?
A: Outliers — squaring the difference makes large errors dominate. Compare to MAE (mean absolute error), which treats all errors linearly.

---

Q: What is KL divergence?
A: Kullback-Leibler divergence. A number measuring how different two probability distributions P and Q are, written `KL(P || Q)`. Zero when P = Q; grows as P diverges from Q.

---

Q: Is KL divergence symmetric?
A: No — `KL(P || Q) ≠ KL(Q || P)` in general. The direction expresses which distribution is the reference. `KL(P || Q)` heavily penalizes P putting mass where Q assigns near zero.

---

Q: What is symmetric KL?
A: The average of both directions: `0.5 * (KL(P||Q) + KL(Q||P))`. Used when neither distribution is ground truth, so both sides are equally penalized for disagreement. Phase IX MICOA uses this.

---

Q: What is asymmetric (one-sided) KL?
A: `KL(P || Q)` where one side (Q) is treated as ground truth and detached from gradients. Only P is updated. Used in Phase X/XII: vision is pulled toward proprio's future, not vice versa.

---

Q: What does `.detach()` do in PyTorch?
A: Removes a tensor from the computation graph so no gradients flow back through it. Used to say "treat this as a constant for the purposes of this loss."

---

Q: What is the Adam optimizer?
A: A widely used gradient-descent variant that adapts the learning rate per-parameter using running averages of the gradient and its square. Default optimizer in SB3.

---

Q: What is over-parametrization (in this RL context)?
A: When a network has far more weights than the task requires. The MICOA actor has ~3.2M weights for a 16-dim action space; most are unused. Helps optimization but makes interpretability harder.

---

## Sigma, clamps, and encoder collapse

Q: What is σ (sigma) in the encoder context?
A: The standard deviation of the encoder's output Gaussian. Each modality (proprio, vision) outputs a distribution `N(μ, σ)` over a shared latent space Z, where σ tells the system how confident that modality is about its own prediction.

---

Q: What does "clamp" mean in optimization?
A: A hard bound enforced on a value. `clamp(x, lo, hi) = min(max(x, lo), hi)` — x cannot escape the interval [lo, hi]. Used to prevent variables from drifting into pathological ranges.

---

Q: What is a σ-clamp (sigma clamp)?
A: A floor enforced on σ so the encoder cannot claim infinite precision. Without a clamp σ can shrink toward zero and the modality dominates Bayesian fusion with potentially noisy content. R40 used σ_p_min = 0.135.

---

Q: What is the "σ-clamp floor"?
A: The minimum value σ is allowed to take. In R40 this floor was 0.135 (raised from R38's 0.018). Floor here = lower bound, the same meaning as "interest rate floor" or "price floor."

---

Q: Why does σ shrinking matter in a Product-of-Experts fusion?
A: PoE weights each modality by its precision (1/σ²). Very small σ means very high precision, which dominates the fused posterior. If the small σ comes with degenerate content, the actor gets dominated by misinformation.

---

Q: What is encoder collapse?
A: When the encoder's σ drops near zero while it claims it can perfectly predict — but actually cannot. Diagnostic signature: σ near 0 AND `kl_pred` huge (the encoder is overconfident in wrong predictions). The Bayesian precision-weighting then gives it dominant influence.

---

Q: What causes encoder collapse?
A: Predictive-KL training pressure can drive the encoder to claim ever-higher precision (smaller σ) faster than it can actually learn to predict. If the σ-clamp floor is too low or training pressure too strong, σ collapses while content stays degenerate. Phase XII under moving balls is the example.

---

Q: How do you diagnose encoder collapse from training logs?
A: Look for `sigma_combined` dropping below the clamp floor (means the clamp lost the fight) and `kl_pred` exploding (R41: 638 vs. healthy 0.6–1.1). High ablation L2 in the presence of these is pathological, not productive integration.

---

Q: What is the Goldilocks σ band?
A: The range of σ-clamp values where the encoder is constrained enough to stay healthy but not so constrained that vision becomes inert. R40 found [0.135, 7.4] works on static balls. Phase XII showed this band is task-dependent, not universal.

---

Q: How did Phase XII refute the "Goldilocks σ band is universal" hypothesis?
A: R41 used the same clamp R40 used (σ_p_min=0.135) but trained on moving balls. σ_combined collapsed to 0.0999 (below the clamp floor) and kl_pred exploded to 638. The clamp that held on static balls did not hold on moving balls.

---

Q: What is "kl_pred" / "kl_pred_k1"?
A: The Phase X/XI/XII predictive KL loss value: `KL(vision(t) || proprio(t+1).detach())`. Healthy range is 0.5–2. R38 pathology was ~116. R41 catastrophe was 638.

---

## MICOA architecture

Q: What does MICOA stand for?
A: Multiple Inputs Confirming One Another. The architecture introduced in Phase IX to address the chronic failure of vision being non-load-bearing across 7 prior substrates.

---

Q: Why was MICOA needed?
A: Concatenating proprio and pixels into one flat vector for SAC is "welding two tubes end-to-end" — SAC just follows whichever gradient is easier (proprio) and the pixel encoder gets no useful signal. MICOA adds a component whose job is to detect when two independent streams are constraining the same world-state.

---

Q: What is Product of Experts (PoE)?
A: A Bayesian-fusion math at the core of MICOA. Each modality outputs a Gaussian over a shared latent; PoE fuses them via precision-weighted averaging. The fused σ shrinks when both agree (confirmation) and stays large when they disagree (uncertainty). Parameter-free.

---

Q: What is the PoE fusion formula for μ_combined?
A: `μ_combined = (μ_p/σ_p² + μ_v/σ_v²) / (1/σ_p² + 1/σ_v²)` — a precision-weighted average of the two modalities' means.

---

Q: What is the PoE fusion formula for σ_combined²?
A: `σ_combined² = 1 / (1/σ_p² + 1/σ_v²)` — the inverse of the summed precisions.

---

Q: What is the MICOAExtractor?
A: The SB3-compatible feature extractor that implements PoE. Each modality has its own encoder (3-layer MLP for proprio, 4-conv DrQ-v2-style CNN for stereo pixels); each outputs (μ, σ); PoE fuses them. Downstream policy sees `[z_combined | μ_p | μ_v]` = 192 dims.

---

Q: What is MICOASAC?
A: A SAC subclass that adds the MICOA agreement loss on top of SAC's normal actor/critic gradients. After SAC's normal backward pass, it does an extra forward+backward over the encoder using a separate Adam optimizer.

---

Q: What is the symmetric KL agreement loss (Phase IX)?
A: `loss_sym = β_sym * 0.5 * (KL(p||v) + KL(v||p))` — both encoders are equally penalized for outputting different distributions about the same observation. Weighted by `--micoa-beta`.

---

Q: What is the temporal predictive KL loss (Phase X onward)?
A: `loss_pred = β_pred * KL(N(μ_v(t), σ_v(t)) || N(μ_p(t+1), σ_p(t+1)).detach())` — vision at t is pulled toward proprio's future at t+1. Asymmetric, one-sided. Weighted by `--micoa-pred-beta`.

---

Q: Why does the predictive KL use `.detach()` on proprio's future?
A: To make the loss one-sided. Only vision is updated by it; proprio is treated as the ground truth for what vision should predict. Mirroring proprio(t) won't satisfy this loss because in general proprio(t) ≠ proprio(t+1).

---

Q: What is the "corner vs tube" framing?
A: A flat MLP over `[proprio | pixels]` makes a longer tube. Two encoders + PoE makes a corner: σ_combined shrinks when both agree, which makes the corner rigid. The "corner" = constrained shared latent; the "tube" = an unconstrained concatenation.

---

Q: What are σ_combined and kl_agreement?
A: MICOA's two main diagnostic scalars, logged every 500 steps. σ_combined falling = corner is forming. kl_agreement near zero = encoder distributions overlap.

---

Q: How do you read σ_combined and kl_agreement together?
A: σ_combined falling while kl_agreement stays moderate-and-falling = healthy corner formation. σ_combined falling while kl_agreement crashes to zero in the first 4K steps = forced collapse (β too high; what R36 did).

---

Q: What is "confirmation without anticipation"?
A: Phase IX's failure mode: under symmetric KL, vision learned to mirror proprio (the cheapest way to satisfy "agree about now"), so vision encoded redundant info and stayed behaviorally inert.

---

Q: What is "anticipation" in MICOA?
A: Phase X's intervention: vision must predict what proprio will encode one step in the future. Mirroring proprio(t) won't satisfy this — vision has to actually anticipate change, which forces it to encode information proprio doesn't yet have.

---

Q: What is the ablation delta?
A: The L2 norm of `action(full_obs) - action(blind_obs)` — how much the policy's action changes when pixels are zeroed. The single primary metric for "vision is load-bearing." Threshold: > 0.05 = passes the dead zone.

---

Q: What is the "vision dead zone"?
A: The ablation delta range 0.009–0.023 that 9 vision experiments hit across Phase VII/VIII/IX. Vision is structurally present but the policy has learned to route around it.

---

Q: What is the "active vs productive" distinction in vision integration?
A: A policy can be "active" on vision (action changes when pixels zeroed → high ablation) but "not productive" (outcomes don't improve). Phase X R38 and Phase XII R41 are both active-but-unproductive.

---

Q: What did Phase IX R36 reveal about MICOA's symmetric agreement loss?
A: With β too high, kl_agreement crashed to ~0 in the first 4K steps — vision collapsed to a copy of proprio. Vision was satisfied (the easy way), so it learned nothing distinctive and stayed inert.

## Body design — what worked and what didn't

Q: Why does the body have wheels instead of legs?
A: Bipedal walking is an entire research problem unto itself. Wheels give locomotion without the distraction of balance, leaving experiments focused on perception.

---

Q: Why is the wheelbase wider than the chassis?
A: So tipping onto any non-bottom face elevates the wheels and tipping is no longer mechanically stable. The "pancake torso" v1 body had a flatter-than-wheelbase chassis and got stuck "on its back."

---

Q: Why are the arms "baby arms" (short and weak)?
A: To match a toddler's arm/body ratio. Long arms with strong gear (the prior v2 sideways arms) levered the chassis off its wheels under random action. Short + weak arms can't lever a 3.6 kg chassis.

---

Q: Why is the head centered (y=0)?
A: A forward-biased head (y=0.08) created a fore-aft mass asymmetry. Every trained policy then moved strictly backward (~0.34 m every seed) because pushing arms forward was mechanically easier. Centering removed this bias.

---

Q: Why are the arms forward-pointing (+Y)?
A: To make shoulder_roll (axis X) swing the arm in the Y-Z plane — forward, down under the body, back. This produces a paddle/rowboat stroke. Sideways arms had a useless shoulder_roll (rotation around its own axis).

---

Q: What is the "any-body-to-target contact counts" rule?
A: A torso, hand, or wheel touching the target all count as a successful contact, not just the hands. Matches Taylor's frame (contact is contact) and fixed a bug where pinning the ball with the torso got no credit.

---

Q: Why does the camera have only 25° FOV and single eye?
A: One camera is the minimum "eye" needed to test "learning to look." Binocular vision, peripheral vision, eye-saccades — all are claims about what the creature needs. Start minimal; let the environment force the rest.

---

Q: What was the rolling-wheels fix (v8-final)?
A: Sliding-sphere wheels with friction 0.05 produced 0.19 m in 20 s — chassis was skidding. Each wheel became a child body with a ball joint and friction was raised to 2.0. Result: 9/20 touches, mean speed 7.46 cm/s, max 21 cm/s (matches a baby crawl).

---

Q: What is the "philosophical point" about the body's joints?
A: Every joint included is a claim about what the creature needs. The goal is to make as few claims as possible and let the environment force the rest.

---

## The simulation stack

Q: What is MuJoCo?
A: A fast, accurate, open-source rigid-body physics simulator (owned by DeepMind). Handles arm dynamics, contacts, gravity. Standard in robot-arm RL literature.

---

Q: What is Gymnasium?
A: The standard Python interface for RL environments (`env.reset()`, `env.step(action)`, etc.). Fork of OpenAI's old `gym`. Project's envs inherit from `gym.Env`.

---

Q: What is Stable-Baselines3 (SB3)?
A: A Python library of clean, tested RL algorithm implementations (including SAC). Used here instead of writing SAC from scratch.

---

Q: What is FPS in training logs?
A: Environment steps per second — how many "advance physics one timestep" cycles complete per real second. Stage 1 (proprio): ~5000 FPS. Stage 2 (vision): ~200 FPS. Vision is slow because rendering pixels costs CPU/GPU.

---

Q: What is proprioception ("proprio")?
A: The sense of your own body's position — joint angles, joint velocities, touch bits, hip pose. Distinct from vision (camera image) and exteroception broadly. In this env: a 7- to 71-dim vector depending on configuration.

---

Q: What is `hide_target_offset`?
A: An env flag. When True, proprio drops the 3-dim fingertip-to-target vector so the agent can't cheat by reading target location from proprio. It must see it or feel it on contact.

---

Q: What does "blind proprio" mean here?
A: The agent has proprio but the camera is off (or there's no camera in the env). Used as a baseline to ask whether vision actually contributes anything in a follow-on stage.

---

Q: What is the FlattenVisionWrapper?
A: A tiny wrapper turning a dict observation `{proprio:[7], vision:[64,64,3]}` into a flat vector of length 7 + 12288 = 12295. Lets you use SB3's standard MlpPolicy.

---

Q: What is a "follow-on" architecture (v2/v3/v5)?
A: Stage 1 is trained first on proprio only, then frozen. Stage 2 ("the follow-on") adds vision on top — only the new pixel-input columns train. Proprio behavior is mathematically preserved (drift = 0).

---

Q: What is the consistency loss (v5)?
A: `λ · MSE(hidden1(full_obs), hidden1(pixels_zeroed_obs))` — forces whatever vision contributes to hidden1 to stay close to what proprio alone would have produced. "Vision must confirm, not reshape."

---

## Representation-analysis tools

Q: What is R² ("R-squared")?
A: Coefficient of determination, range [−∞, 1]. R²=1 means the regressor predicts targets perfectly. R²=0 = no better than guessing the mean. Negative = worse than guessing the mean.

---

Q: What is a linear probe?
A: A simple test: freeze the model, train a linear readout to predict X from the model's hidden activations, measure R². If R² is high, the info is there and easily extractable. Low R² could mean info is absent OR tangled non-linearly.

---

Q: What is ridge regression?
A: Linear regression with a small penalty on large weights to prevent overfitting. The specific linear-probe regressor used in this project (`sklearn.linear_model.Ridge`).

---

Q: What is the difference between "representation failure" and "policy failure"?
A: Two ways a channel can be active-but-useless. Representation failure = the latent never encoded the task variable (a probe can't decode it above chance) → fix the encoder. Policy failure = the latent DOES encode it (probe R² meaningful) but the policy never learned to act on it → fix the RL update. The ball-x probe found representation failure: R43's vision latent decodes ball direction at R²≈0.08 (below the proprio control), so its high ablation is binding to non-directional features, not a usable spatial code.

---

Q: What is CKA (Centered Kernel Alignment)?
A: A similarity measure between two sets of activations from different networks on the same inputs. CKA=1 = essentially the same representation (up to linear reshuffling). CKA=0 = totally unrelated.

---

Q: Why does CKA matter for interpenetration?
A: If v5's hidden1 has high CKA with stage1_v3's hidden1, vision is living on proprio's manifold — additive interpenetration. Low CKA = vision has carved out its own manifold and ignored proprio's.

---

Q: What is k-NN (k-nearest neighbors)?
A: For each point, find its k closest neighbors (this project: k=5, Euclidean distance in hidden1 space). Used as a probe: if two observations are neighbors in hidden1, are their actions also similar?

---

Q: What is neighbor-consistency ratio?
A: `mean(action-distance among hidden1 neighbors) / mean(action-distance among random pairs)`. Lower = tighter equivalence classes. Stage1_v3 = 0.07 (14× tighter than random); v1 agents = ~0.45.

---

Q: What is k-means?
A: An unsupervised clustering algorithm. Given N points and a k, finds k centers such that each observation is assigned to its nearest center. Used to carve hidden1 activations into discrete Π-classes for inspection.

---

Q: What is silhouette score?
A: Cleanliness of a clustering, range [-1, 1]. High = points much closer to own cluster's center than to other centers; low = clusters bleed into each other.

---

Q: What is Spearman correlation?
A: Correlation between the ranks of two variables, not raw values. Robust to outliers and to non-linear-but-monotonic relationships.

---

## Source theory (Taylor / interpenetration)

Q: What is set-theoretic perception theory?
A: Taylor's framework: perception is a *response* shaped by what the world conditions, not a model the experimenter writes in. The project's grounding theory.

---

Q: What is "pressure not bribery"?
A: A methodological rule: when you want the creature to learn behavior X, change the environment so X becomes instrumental, rather than paying reward for X directly. Pressure forces emergence; bribery just trains a habit.

---

Q: What is an equivalence class (in this theory)?
A: Taylor's central construct. Many different sensory states map to the same conditioned response. "The class IS the perception." Neighbor-consistency ratio is a proxy for it.

---

Q: What is Π (Pi) in Taylor's notation?
A: A readiness state: the set of conditioned responses an organism is currently prepared to execute. Perception in Taylor's theory is just the simultaneous pattern of Π.

---

Q: What is interpenetration (Ch. 5 in Taylor)?
A: "Properties of the perceptual field determined by one set are incorporated in the perceptual field determined by the other set." Operationally: vision's representation should inherit and extend proprio's, not overwrite it.

---

Q: What is an unconditioned response?
A: Reflexive, built-in (e.g., contact → grasp). The starting set of behaviors before learning.

---

Q: What is a conditioned response?
A: Learned to fire from a previously-neutral cue (e.g., the *sight* of an object → grasp, after learning that seeing precedes touching).

---

Q: What is critical state D in Taylor's framework?
A: The drive state — what creates the pressure to act (e.g., contact drive in this project's task).

---

Q: What is sensory surface Q?
A: The set of receptor signals available to the organism. Vision, proprio, touch — all components of Q.

---

Q: What is M in Taylor's framework?
A: A motor behavior (e.g., forward-paddle). The Phase VIII hypothesis was that pure-paddle M had to fail unless modified by visual evidence.

---

## The phase history (chronological)

Q: What was Phase 0 (Stage 0)?
A: Flat floor, no fall penalty, wide targets, scaffolded. Result with blind proprio: 6/20 touches, 0 falls. Establishes baseline locomotion.

---

Q: What was Stage 1?
A: 2x2 m platform, fall penalty, gaze-pressure cone exclusion. Warm-started from Stage 0; peaked at 7/20 touches, 0 falls. First sign of late-training regression.

---

Q: What is "late-training regression"?
A: Best-checkpoint and final-checkpoint differ by ~5x in touch rate. SAC over-exploits and loses the plateau. Fixes considered: entropy floor, early stopping, lower learning rate.

---

Q: What was the "one-direction motion" problem?
A: Blind proprio has no input telling it where the ball is, so it learns one canned paddle motion and hopes the ball is in that direction. About 1/3 of placements happen to be within that cone.

---

Q: What was Phase I (mimo_substrate_A)?
A: No-bribery substrate using existing CNN. Result: no motion at all. Auto-entropy collapsed the policy to zero-action.

---

Q: What was Phase II (mimo_dialogue_v1)?
A: Dialogue architecture on the no-bribery substrate. Same failure family.

---

Q: What were Phases III / III.2?
A: Fixed-ball-position substrates. Still hit the "no motion at all" failure mode.

---

Q: What was Phase IV (HER)?
A: Hindsight Experience Replay + fixed balls + memory + small velocity bonus. First HER attempt; affected by a wrapper bug that zeroed the velocity bonus.

---

Q: What was the her_wrapper.py bug?
A: HER relabels 4/5 of sampled transitions and runs `compute_reward` on them — those transitions saw only the sparse term, so the velocity bonus only flowed through 1/5 of gradients. Effective velocity bonus = 0 for Phases IV and V.

---

Q: What was Phase V / V.2?
A: Caregiver scaffold + velocity bonus runs. V.2 was first time HER left the −2000 floor (mean reward −1899). First non-zero eval std in months.

---

Q: What was Phase VI / VI.2?

## Concepts the user asked to clarify

Q: What is "gating" in a neural network context?
A: A learned soft switch that decides how much each input pathway contributes (or whether it contributes at all). LSTMs have forget/input/output gates. "Gated" generally means "controlled by another signal."

---

Q: Does "gated" mean "restricted"?
A: Approximately yes — gating restricts or filters flow. In a neural net it's typically multiplicative: output = (gate_value) × (signal), where gate_value is between 0 and 1. Gate=0 blocks; gate=1 passes; in between attenuates.

---

Q: What is gaze-gated reward?
A: A reward structure where reward credit is only awarded when the agent has *visually attended* to the target before contact. The "gate" is the gaze: no gaze, no reward. Forces vision to become behaviorally necessary.

---

Q: Why is gaze-gated reward listed as a Phase XIII candidate?
A: Because all the architectural interventions (PoE, predictive KL, MICOA) still let the actor route around vision. Gaze-gating ties the reward signal itself to visual attention — vision must be used or there is no credit. It directly addresses the "vision active but not productive" failure.

---

Q: Is gaze-gated reward "bribery" or "pressure"?
A: This is open. The project's "pressure not bribery" rule says don't pay reward for desired behavior. A gaze gate doesn't pay for gaze — it restricts credit on other behavior unless gaze occurred. Closer to pressure (changing what's instrumental) than bribery (paying for the act).

---

Q: What is DrQ-v2?
A: Data-regularized Q-learning v2 — an image-aware RL algorithm that augments visual inputs (random shifts) during Q-learning, and is known to make vision-based SAC more sample-efficient. Listed as a Phase XIII candidate.

---

Q: What does "image-aware RL" mean?
A: RL algorithms designed with vision in mind — e.g., data augmentation on pixels, contrastive visual losses, image-specific architectures. Plain SAC + CNN is NOT image-aware; it treats pixels like any other input.

---

Q: What is the "one-armed body" candidate?
A: A Phase XIII body modification where geometric reach cannot cover the entire arena from a fixed posture. Removes proprio's self-sufficient "paddle-and-hope" strategy and forces directional steering — which requires sensing where the ball is, i.e., vision.

---

## Vision load-bearing — the central question

Q: What does "vision load-bearing" mean operationally?
A: When the policy's action changes meaningfully on the same state if pixel input is zeroed. Measured by the L2 norm of `action(full_obs) − action(blind_obs)`. The strict criterion is delta > 0.05.

---

Q: What's the difference between "vision present" and "vision load-bearing"?
A: Present = the CNN exists in the network and gradients flow through it. Load-bearing = the policy's actual outputs depend on what the camera shows. For 9 prior experiments vision was present but not load-bearing.

---

Q: Why was vision not load-bearing across 7 substrates (R6, R10, R14/R15, R17/R18, R26/R27, R28/R29, R32-R35)?
A: Plain SAC + concatenated proprio + pixels follows whichever gradient is easier (always proprio in this body), and the pixel encoder gets no useful training signal. Architecture, not task, was the bottleneck.

---

Q: What's the "ornamental CNN" diagnosis?
A: A CNN that exists in the network but has no behavioral influence — its gradients have been suppressed by the actor learning to rely on proprio. Visible as ablation delta < 0.025.

---

Q: What was the 2026-05-07 breakthrough?
A: micoa_freeze_zeroinit_cone30 best checkpoint: ablation delta 0.456, 100% of steps non-trivial. First confirmed vision-load-bearing run in the project's history (after 5+ runs with near-zero sensitivity).

---

Q: What did the 2026-05-07 breakthrough NOT show?
A: That vision was steering correctly. Directional spawn test: ball fixed 15° right → 0/30 touches; ball fixed 15° left → 0/30 touches. Vision was influencing actions but had no mapping from "ball location" → "correct turn."

---

Q: What is "vision affects behavior" vs "vision affects behavior correctly"?
A: Different questions. The first asks whether pixel inputs change actions (load-bearing). The second asks whether those changed actions consistently move the agent toward the target (productive). Phase XII: yes to (1), no to (2).

## Failure modes (the taxonomy)

Q: What is "no motion at all"?
A: A failure mode from Phases I, II, III, III.2: auto-entropy collapse to a zero-action deterministic policy. The creature stops moving entirely.

---

Q: What is "one move then freeze"?
A: A failure mode from Phases IV, V.2, VI, VI.2: the policy commits to one initial action then freezes. Not entropy collapse (ent_coef pin holds) — it's a rational SAC equilibrium under low pressure.

---

Q: What is "geometric refuge"?
A: When the spawn distribution accidentally puts the agent within touching distance of the ball without doing anything. The policy collects free reward by sitting still. Phase V.2 had this on the inside of the goal-threshold geometry.

---

Q: What is the "active but not productive" failure?
A: Phase X R38 and Phase XII R41. Vision is integrated into the policy (high ablation delta), but the integrated information doesn't steer behavior in a useful direction. Outcomes don't improve.

---

Q: What is "pathological integration"?
A: When the encoder σ collapses while kl_pred explodes. The pixel pathway dominates the actor's input (high ablation) but the content is degenerate. R41 is the canonical example.

---

Q: What is the dependency chain failure?
A: body → motion → encounter → memory → map. Most phases broke at link 2 (motion). Phase VII+ resolved motion via the cart substrate but the chain still broke at link 4 (memory) or link 5 (map).

## Common reward-design concepts

Q: What is "reward shaping"?
A: Adding extra reward terms beyond the sparse goal signal to guide learning (e.g., +0.10 per cm/s of forward velocity). The project's "pressure not bribery" rule says shaping is bribery unless it makes a non-target behavior instrumental.

---

Q: What is HER (Hindsight Experience Replay)?
A: A technique where failed transitions are relabeled with the goal "the agent actually reached," not the goal originally specified. Helps with sparse rewards. The project's her_wrapper.py implements it.

---

Q: What is the velocity bonus?
A: A small reward term proportional to torso speed (e.g., scale × |hip_velocity|). Used to discourage stillness. Phase VI's debate was whether it survives HER's 4/5 relabel dilution.

---

Q: What is the goal threshold?
A: The distance at which a contact counts as "reached." If the ball spawns inside the threshold, the agent gets free reward by doing nothing — a geometric refuge.

## Bayesian fusion concepts

Q: What is a Gaussian?
A: A bell-curve probability distribution, fully specified by its mean μ (center) and standard deviation σ (spread). Written `N(μ, σ)`. The most-used distribution in ML because it has clean math (closed-form KL, easy sampling, simple precision-weighted fusion).

---

Q: Why do MICOA encoders output Gaussians instead of point estimates?
A: A point estimate says "the latent is here" with no confidence info. A Gaussian says "I think it's here (μ), and I'm this sure (σ)." The PoE fusion needs the σ to weight modalities by precision — without it there's no way to know whose claim should dominate.

---

Q: What is precision (in Bayesian terms)?
A: The inverse variance: precision = 1/σ². "How confident am I in this estimate?" High precision = small σ = sharp claim.

---

Q: How does precision-weighted averaging work?
A: Each input is weighted by its claimed precision. High-precision (small-σ) inputs dominate the combined estimate. Bayes-optimal when the inputs are conditionally independent.

---

Q: Why does PoE multiply distributions instead of adding?
A: Multiplying two Gaussians gives a Gaussian whose precision is the sum of the precisions — exactly Bayesian combination of independent estimates. Adding gives a mixture, which represents disjunctive uncertainty, not joint confirmation.

---

Q: What is a posterior in Bayesian terms?
A: The updated belief about a variable after seeing evidence: posterior = (likelihood × prior) / evidence. The PoE fused (μ_combined, σ_combined) is the posterior over the latent given both modalities.

---

Q: What is multisensory integration in Bayesian terms?
A: Combining multiple sensory inputs about the same world state using their relative precisions. Reduces uncertainty when modalities agree; preserves uncertainty when they disagree. The brain is theorized to do this.

## Generalization, robustness & recent methods (Phase XII–XV)

Q: What is DroQ?
A: Dropout Q-functions — a tweak to SAC's critics (dropout + layer-norm inside the critic, plus a higher update-to-data ratio). The regularization lets you do many gradient updates per env step without the critic diverging, so the agent learns more per sample. Introduced Phase XIV (R45), run with --droq --dropout-rate 0.01 --utd 4.

---

Q: What is UTD (update-to-data ratio)?
A: How many gradient updates the agent does per environment step. Vanilla SAC is UTD=1; DroQ lets us push to UTD=4 for faster learning in samples. Only stable with regularization like DroQ.

---

Q: What is eccentricity (ecc) in our evals?
A: How far off-center the target is from straight ahead, in meters of lateral offset. ecc=0.00 = ball dead ahead (easiest); larger = further to the side (harder). Standard bins: 0.00, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25.

---

Q: Why is the eccentricity sweep the only valid health metric on this substrate?
A: The Phase XIV methodological finding: eval mean_reward and critic_loss are invalid here (cart motion and reward shaping make them misleading). Only the deterministic both-touched success at each ecc bin (eval_phase_v.py) reliably reflects behavior.

---

Q: What is vision-ablation sensitivity (abl_L2)?
A: Zero out the pixel columns of the observation and measure how much the policy's action vector changes (L2). High = policy depends on vision; near-zero (~0.002–0.024 "dead zone") = vision inert. Preferred over task-success because AB can solve the task by proprio-grope alone.

---

Q: Does high vision-ablation mean vision is useful?
A: No. R41 had the highest ablation ever (0.85) yet performed worse than the proprio control. High action-level ablation = "load-bearing"; actually winning more episodes than a proprio control = "productive." Vision keeps clearing the first bar without the second.

---

Q: What is "generalization-as-primary"?
A: The post-Phase-V reframing: the interesting claim isn't "vision helps" but that AB builds a representation that generalizes — lawful behavior on configurations it never trained on. Acid test = zero-shot transfer to held-out objects. Core confirmed for proprio; strong form (vision recruited at the margin) refuted.

---

Q: What is zero-shot transfer, in Phase XV terms?
A: Performance on objects AB was never trained on, with no extra training. Phase XV trains on a subset of an equivalence class (e.g. ball radii {0.040, 0.053, 0.075}) and measures both-touched on held-out members ({0.047, 0.090}).

---

Q: Interpolation vs extrapolation for a held-out value?
A: Interpolation = the held-out value sits inside the trained range (0.047 between 0.040 and 0.075). Extrapolation = outside it (0.090 beyond 0.075). Extrapolation is the harder, more telling test of a metric representation.

---

Q: What is the distance law / time-to-contact finding?
A: Steps-to-reach scales lawfully with target distance (R44 proprio ≈ 688·d − 218, r≈0.89) and extrapolates to untrained distances — evidence the representation is metric, not memorized. Confound: the sweeping cart logs spurious ~1-step contacts that add scatter.

---

Q: What is the equivalence-class test (object variety)?
A: The Phase XV paradigm: treat "ball" as a class with members varying along a dimension (size, shape), train on some, hold others out, and ask whether AB treats held-out members as the same class (still reaches them). Operationalizes the source theory's equivalence-class construct.

---

Q: What is "the 0.075 anomaly"?
A: A reproducible dip in both-touched success at the TRAINED ball radius 0.075 (~6/20) in both R46 and R48, even though smaller and larger sizes do better. Anomalous because a trained size should be easy; flagged for a focused diagnostic.

---

Q: What is the constant_velocity_bouncer cart mode?
A: The substrate AB sits on — a cart drifting at constant speed (--cart-speed 0.15) that bounces off the arena walls, giving gentle ongoing motion. Distinct from a static base or a self-moving ball (--ball-speed, set to 0 in Phase XIV/XV).

---

Q: What is the difference between broad touch and hand-only touch in the cart env?
A: Broad (`touched_ball*`) fires on a collision between the ball and ANY MIMo geom (feet/legs/torso/head/hands); hand-only (`hand_touched_ball*`) counts only the 8 hand/finger geoms. Reward, termination, and "both-touched" success use the BROAD metric — success need not involve a hand.

---

Q: What did the Phase XV hand-touch diagnostic find?
A: both_HAND = 0 across every size AND every ecc bin, on the whole cart line (R44/R45/R46/R48) — the two-ball task is never COMPLETED with two hands. But hands DO engage for one ball (any_HAND up to 21/30), rising with eccentricity where a real reach is needed. Verified not a detection bug (8 hand geoms correctly identified). Reframe: both_HAND=0 is a developmental stage, not a bug.

---

Q: What is cart-sweep delivery, and why does it matter?
A: The balls sit on the cart's sweep line, so the moving cart carries AB's BODY into the stationary ball — the substrate delivers the ball rather than AB reaching. (The cart geom itself is a non-colliding visual marker; it never pushes the ball.) It's why low-ecc success is hand-free. Not purely a confound — incidental body contact is a valid "world-is-consistent" signal; but to study reaching, move balls off the sweep line or score hand-only.

---

Q: What actually causes the "0.075 anomaly"?
A: A glancing-blow knock-away. A mid-size ball (~0.070-0.080) is struck off-centre and skids to the arena wall, out of the no-locomotion creature's reach; the second ball can't be recovered so the episode never completes. It's a contiguous dead BAND, not a point; geometric (mass unchanged by radius); a manipulation artifact, not a generalization hole.

---

Q: Why can't you trust describe_video.py (Gemini) for pass/fail on these runs?
A: It narrated the failing 0.075 episodes as successful "throw-then-pickup" sequences — it doesn't know the task is "touch both balls", so it over-reported success. Pass/fail must come from the metric and from frames read directly, not the free-text narration.

## Encoder / decoder and prior-work concepts

Q: In our context, what is an encoder?
A: The part of the network that turns raw sensory input into the compact internal latent the policy reads. We have two (one per modality): the proprio encoder (3-layer MLP, 7 proprio numbers → 64-dim Gaussian (μ,σ) over the shared latent Z) and the vision encoder (DrQ-v2 4-conv CNN, stereo image → its own 64-dim (μ,σ)). "The visual latent" we keep probing IS the vision encoder's output. "Representation failure" = the encoder's output doesn't carry the needed info; the encoder is the fix target when the problem is representational.

---

Q: In our context, what is a decoder?
A: A readout that runs the other way — from the internal latent back to an interpretable quantity. We don't keep one as a permanent part of the agent; we attach them in two roles: (1) a PROBE (read-only diagnostic) — the ball-x linear probe is a decoder, latent → ball lateral position, R²≈0.08 measured the representation failure; (2) an AUXILIARY LOSS — a trainable decoder whose error is pushed back into the encoder to force it to encode the target. Read-only decoder measures the failure; auxiliary-loss decoder tries to fix it.

---

Q: What is "modality dominance / modality competition"?
A: From supervised multimodal learning (Wang/Tran/Feiszli 2020; Peng OGM-GE 2022): trained jointly on two streams, the easier-to-learn stream wins the gradient and the other stays underused — a joint net can lose to the best single modality. It's the closest NAMED neighbor to AB's "vision integrated but inert" (proprio solves the task, so vision is starved). Difference: in that literature the weak modality is usually recoverable by rebalancing; AB's is more severe (the latent barely encodes the target — representation failure).

---

Q: What is privileged teacher → student distillation?
A: The field's standard fix for making vision load-bearing when a non-visual channel already solves the task (Learning by Cheating 2019; RMA 2021; Lee/Hwangbo 2020). Train a teacher with privileged info unavailable at deployment (e.g. the exact target vector), then train a vision-only student to imitate the teacher's actions. Vision becomes load-bearing because supervision forces it — the step AB's end-to-end run skips. The #1 recognized fix for AB's pathology.

---

Q: What is an asymmetric actor-critic?
A: An RL trick (Pinto et al. 2018) where the critic gets extra privileged info (full state) during training while the actor sees only the real sensor observations. The critic is discarded at deployment, so feeding it ground-truth doesn't cheat at test time but gives the actor a better learning signal. "Asymmetric" = actor and critic see different things. A gentler alternative to full teacher→student distillation for putting supervisory pressure on a vision policy.

---

Q: What is an auxiliary (ball-position) decode loss?
A: A second objective bolted onto the RL loss whose only job is to force the encoder to represent a specific variable. Attach a small decoder to the vision latent, train it to predict ball position, and backprop that error INTO the encoder — so the encoder is rewarded for encoding ball position regardless of whether the task gradient does. AB's #2 candidate fix; targets the encoder directly (vs the read-only probe, which only measures the failure). Success test: does vision-ablation become directional (which side the ball is on) instead of the content-free ecc=0 spike?

---

Q: Is generalization just "many variations resolving to the same outcome"? (domain randomization vs equivalence class)
A: That's the mechanism (called domain randomization in robotics RL, and the equivalence class in the source theory: many instances -> one conditioned response). But variation is NECESSARY, not SUFFICIENT. Proof from our own runs: proprio saw the variations and built a lawful, extrapolating class (the distance law); vision saw the SAME variations and still failed (R^2~=0.08) because nothing forced its latent to encode what varied. Variation only generalizes when the learner is compelled to REPRESENT it. Also note: in RL the "same outcome" is defined by the reward function, standing in for the body's unconditioned reflex in development.

---

Q: What is the "coordination vs interpenetration" distinction (Numenta vs Taylor/MICOA)?
A: COORDINATION (Numenta voting): each cortical column builds its own object model independently; modules exchange messages and reach consensus, but no module's internal representation is restructured by another's learning history -- experts vote, the vote doesn't rewire any expert. INTERPENETRATION (Taylor Ch.5, what MICOA builds): the modules' representations become mutually constitutive -- vision comes to encode the world the way proprio does because proprio's history reshapes vision. The disagreement is NOT about whether a domain-general mechanism exists (Numenta grants one: reference frames everywhere) -- it's about content-merger. Prism adaptation is the discriminating test.

Q: How does prism adaptation discriminate coordination from interpenetration?
A: If vision only COORDINATED with proprio via voting, reversing prisms would produce permanent vision-vs-proprio disagreement (vision votes "left", proprio "right", forever). Instead, over days of active movement, vision RECALIBRATES onto the motor frame -- its internal mapping is restructured by the other modality's learning history. That restructuring is interpenetration; pure coordination cannot produce it.

Q: What does the prism-adaptation literature establish for AB's proprio-first thesis?
A: Two facts. (1) Adaptation requires ACTIVE, self-produced movement -- Held & Hein's 1963 kitten carousel: the passively-carried kitten gets identical visual input and does NOT adapt; exposure isn't enough, action is necessary (the passive kitten = a pure confirmation loss). (2) The motor/world frame is the ANCHOR: when vision and action conflict the system re-anchors vision toward action, not vice versa ("reality and movement don't switch; vision does"). Justifies the .detach()-on-proprio asymmetry in MICOA's temporal-predictive loss.

Q: Does Numenta's "replication beats centralization" argument rule out AB's shared generalization mechanism?
A: No. That argument is against a BASE COLUMN (one privileged module others query) -- which AB doesn't propose. AB proposes a base MECHANISM (a domain-general way of building generalization every column instantiates) -- and Numenta already believes in one (reference frames used everywhere). They agree on mechanism-generality; they disagree only on content-merger (interpenetration). Refinement AB holds honestly: a general mechanism doesn't guarantee automatic success -- AB's vision stayed inert (R^2~=0.08) despite seeing all the variation.

---

Q: What is the "movement-as-substrate" thesis, and what's the open bet in it?
A: A step beyond generalization-as-primary: movement isn't just learned first, it's the SUBSTRATE the generalizing representation is built on. Confirmed for proprio (the extrapolating distance law; prism adaptation showing the motor frame is the anchor). The OPEN, not-yet-confirmed bet: that building in richer movement makes VISION generalize too. R49 and DOF-curriculum runs are the cheap probes of that causal link -- to be confirmed on the current body BEFORE any expensive port to a richer simulator.

Q: What is the rule "faithful to the mechanism, free on the implementation"?
A: AB reconstructs human perception but isn't bound to the human developmental path. Judge each human fact by whether it's LOAD-BEARING for the mechanism (active self-produced movement; motor competence before vision is trusted; graded freezing/freeing of DOF -- keep) or an INCIDENTAL detail of being a mammal (gestation, helpless emergence, the literal year of crawling -- drop or compress into a curriculum). Diverge freely where it SERVES the mechanism (privileged training signals, near-perfect proprioception, repeatable resets, compressed lifetimes); stay faithful only to movement-first, interpenetration, and the equivalence class.

Q: What are the two concrete levers for building movement in as a substrate, and their cost?
A: (1) Graded DOF freezing/freeing (Berthouze & Lungarella) -- lock most joints, master the reduced body, progressively release DOF so each motor competence scaffolds the next; CHEAP, a curriculum change in MuJoCo today. (2) Migrate to a richer/growing body (MIMo v2: hands, foveated vision, sensorimotor delays, birth->24mo growth) -- EXPENSIVE, justified only once the substrate->vision-generalization link is confirmed on the current simplified body.
